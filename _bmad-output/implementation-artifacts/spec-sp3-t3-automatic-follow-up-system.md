## Intent

**Problem:** The baseline processes manually inserted due rows but never creates a reminder automatically, cannot prove one durable silence episode across retries, and does not preserve complete cancellation evidence.

**Approach:** When an approved automated sales reply first reaches durable `sent`, atomically schedule one Follow-Up for that silence episode exactly 12 hours later. A newer qualifying sent reply supersedes the pending reminder; a committed customer reply before provider-call start cancels it. When due, recheck every current rule and either record a typed terminal reason or create one intent through SP3-T2.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:** PostgreSQL owns scheduling and evidence. Bind identity to the source sent intent; retries reuse it. Persist due time, action key/generation, source Conversation version, approved content reference, Release Set/business versions, consent timestamp/hash, and correlation ID. Recheck reply/version, consent, lifecycle/ownership, controls/campaign, timing/frequency, Release Set, and WhatsApp eligibility at due time and provider-call start. Reply-first means zero calls; call-start-first retains one call.

**Ask First:** Any follow-up count/delay change, new customer wording/template metadata, cancellation precedence change, replay of cancelled/ambiguous work, retry or backlog alert thresholds, alert destination/owner, or live provider connection.

**Never:** Use n8n memory/Wait nodes, recurse from a Follow-Up send, treat due time as permission, add an adapter, overwrite outcomes, claim late cancellation prevented a call, promise external exactly-once, or enable live promotion.

## I/O & Edge-Case Matrix

| Scenario | Input / State | Expected Output / Behavior | Error Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| First sent reply | Qualifying reply first becomes `sent` | One job due at sent time +12h | Terminal replay returns the same job |
| Newer sent reply | Older active job exists | One newer active job; older is superseded | Retain both source IDs |
| Reply/call race | Customer inbound races provider-call start | Commit order determines cancelled-with-zero-call or one-started-call | Never report false prevention |
| Due silence | No reply; all current rules permit | One intent follows SP3-T2 to sent/delivered | Concurrent schedulers create no duplicate |
| Prohibition | Reply, opt-out, human/terminal state, stop, timing/frequency/template/release denial | Exact terminal reason; no new call | Missing authority fails closed |
| Upgrade/recovery | Legacy active rows or overdue backlog | Legacy unverifiable work becomes terminal; bounded catch-up | Expose queue/retry/exhaustion metrics |

## Design Notes

The existing `followup-en` key remains the synthetic approved content reference; the human-approved local metadata is provider template `followup-en`, category `marketing`, and language `en`. This story does not invent wording or claim Meta readiness. Backlog count/age/retry/exhausted metrics are in scope, but thresholded alerts remain blocked until thresholds and an Operations owner are approved.

Runtime scheduling must derive eligibility time from PostgreSQL, never a request body. Tests may make fixture jobs due through explicit test setup, but the published scheduler path cannot accept a future business clock. Every authority checked while scheduling or creating an intent must share a deterministic lock order with its writer, and disabled accounts must still be selected for typed terminalization rather than disappearing from the queue.

Qualification precedes supersession: a newer sent reply that is denied must not destroy an older valid reminder. Follow-Up source identity, due time, generation, versions, consent evidence, content reference, correlation, and request hash are immutable after insertion; due execution verifies their hash. Scheduler paths acquire authority locks before row locks, and a bounded pass means the oldest available 50—not a preselected locked prefix. Privacy minimization may redact content but must never rewrite a durable call-start, sent, delivered, failed, or reconciliation outcome as cancelled.

Supersession covers the whole pre-call unit: job and outbound intent transition atomically, while a started call remains honest and non-replayable. Due authorization must finish every current eligibility check before intent insertion, and final authorization must match the persisted consent episode. A scheduler invocation may inspect/lock at most its bounded processable set; contention evidence requires an actual failed lock attempt. All state writers and workers use the same acquisition protocol, including trigger-invoked paths. Deletion may minimize identifiers but must preserve a non-PII callback key sufficient to reconcile an already-started provider call.

Loop 5's acquisition protocol is a testable invariant: one Conversation-scoped advisory key is acquired first on every Follow-Up-linked writer/worker path; no path may hold a Conversation, Follow-Up, or intent row while waiting for that key. Global/config authorities are acquired after the Conversation key in one documented order, and candidate probing is nonblocking so a busy key cannot stall later work. `row_busy` means an observed failed probe, never a cutoff inference. The persisted campaign snapshot and one correlation ID travel from scheduled job through intent, provider call, callback, and audit. Deletion preserves a hashed callback alias solely for already-started reconciliation.

Loop 6 resolves the remaining boundedness ambiguity explicitly: a scheduler transaction retains at most 50 successful claims, keyset-scans past busy candidates until those 50 or true exhaustion, and is wall-clock bounded by a PostgreSQL statement timeout rather than a fixed candidate prefix. Each probe uses transaction-scoped advisory locks inside a rollbackable subtransaction; a failed probe releases every partial lock before continuing. Busy observations are durable but parent-independent so recording them cannot wait on the busy Follow-Up row. Runtime eligibility, leases, and expiry use refreshed PostgreSQL time; caller-supplied time exists only in disposable fixture setup, never as production authority.

Sent completion is a new eligibility decision, not permission inherited from provider-call start. It must prove the exact Conversation version and current account/control/consent/business/template authorities before creating or superseding a reminder, while frequency is deliberately rechecked only at due time and call start because the rolling 24-hour window may change before the +12h due time. A terminal customer-reply cancellation is immutable history and is never relabeled as supersession.
